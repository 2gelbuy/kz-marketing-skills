# Advertising in healthcare: clinics, medicines, medical devices, supplements

Source: Code of the Republic of Kazakhstan "On Public Health and the Healthcare System" (2020, No. 360-VI), Article 56, on Adilet: https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/K2000000360, read 2026-11-17. The article carries amendments by the law of 6 April 2024 No. 71-VIII.

This is a digest of one article. The procedures the article refers to (set by the state bodies for medicines, for sanitary control and for medical services) were not read. Give the rule, name the point, and send the user to a lawyer or the regulator before release.

## Who sets the procedure (p. 1)

| What is advertised | Procedure set by |
|---|---|
| Medicines and medical devices | The state body for circulation of medicines and medical devices |
| Products under sanitary and epidemiological control that need state registration (placing dietary supplements here is the author's reading; p. 3.15 and Art. 428 of the Code "On Administrative Offences" name supplements directly) | The state body for sanitary and epidemiological welfare |
| Medical services | The state body for medical services |

## What every healthcare ad must be (p. 2)

Truthful; recognizable as advertising without special knowledge or tools; free of comparisons with other services, medicines, devices or registered products; not misleading about characteristics, composition, consumer properties, price, expected results of use, or results of research and trials.

## Prohibited (p. 3)

1. Advertising medicines, medical devices, registered products under sanitary control and means of prevention that are not registered in Kazakhstan.
2. Advertising prescription medicines in mass media.
3. Handing out samples of prescription medicines as advertising.
4. Using children, their image or voice in advertising of medicines and medical devices, except those made for children.
5. Advertising medicines, medical devices and registered products under sanitary control in public transport and in organizations unrelated to their purpose, use or dispensing. Medical and pharmaceutical conferences are the exception for medicines.
6. Advertising on industrial products and on prescription forms.
7. Outdoor (visual) advertising of medicines and medical devices.
8. Using medical workers who are authorized to prescribe as distributors of advertising. Truthful information for a scientific or educational purpose, or to inform patients, is the exception.
9. Advertising services without a licence for that activity.
10. Advertising services provided by persons without a healthcare specialist certificate, foreign specialists included.
11. Naming, in advertising for the public, ways of treating sexually transmitted diseases, cancer, mental and behavioural disorders, dangerous infectious diseases, HIV, tuberculosis, diabetes.
12. Referring to recommendations of scientists, healthcare specialists or officials of state bodies that may encourage use or prescription of medicines and medical devices.
13. Presenting services, medicines, medical devices or registered products as unique, the safest or the most effective.
14. Claiming that a medicine is safe and effective because it is of natural origin.
15. Suggesting that the effect of a service, of treatment with the advertised medicine or of a dietary supplement is guaranteed, or that use has no side effects.
16. Information not directly related to the advertised service, medicine or device.
17. Advertising offers of transactions with human organs or tissues.

## Where it may run (p. 4, p. 5)

Advertising of services, medicines and medical devices is allowed in mass media and on digital resources in healthcare organizations (the wording of p. 4; how far "digital resources" reaches is a question for a lawyer). An advertisement for a medicine must contain complete and truthful information, including the restrictions on use.

## Checking a clinic or pharmacy ad

Run the general check from the skill first, then these:

1. Is there a licence for this activity, and are its number and the issuing body in the ad? (p. 3.9 here; Art. 6 p. 4 of the Law "On Advertising")
2. Do the named doctors hold specialist certificates? (p. 3.10)
3. Remove "the best", "unique", "the safest", "No. 1", "guaranteed result", "no side effects", "100%". (p. 3.13, 3.15)
4. Remove comparisons with other clinics, methods or medicines. (p. 2)
5. Does the ad name a way of treating a disease from the list in p. 3.11? Remove it.
6. A doctor recommending a medicine or device in the ad fails p. 3.12; a doctor who prescribes acting as the advertiser's face fails p. 3.8.
7. Medicines and devices: registered in Kazakhstan, not prescription-only in mass media, not outdoors, not in public transport, restrictions on use stated.
8. "Before and after" photos and patient testimonials: the article has no separate rule on them. They fall under "expected results" (p. 2) and "guaranteed effect" (p. 3.15), and in a sponsored post under the consent rule in `online-advertising.md`. Say this is a reading, not a quoted rule.

The fine for advertising medical services without a licence, or supplements without state registration, is in `fines.md` (Art. 428).
